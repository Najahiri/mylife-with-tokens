One example at every step. One: the crawler fetches the raw HTML of a public page, here a Diabetes Center Berne article on its four diabetes technology professorships. Two: the cleaner keeps only the text, menus and footers go, and the text is split into passages of about 160 words and embedded with BGE-M3. That is the offline part. Three: in the chat, the user types a request, for example a start-up developing wearable glucose sensors. Four: Apertus does not search with keywords; it writes short passages the way a matching web page would read, that is HyDE. Five: RAG returns up to thirty best-matching pages, and Apertus reads them in full. Six: Apertus writes an item with a quote copied from the page, and plain code checks that the quote is really there. Seven: the item becomes a slide in the Kanton Bern design, in the preview and as PDF or PowerPoint. Steps one, two and the quote are real, from dcberne.com; the passages and the page list are examples of what Apertus writes and gets back.
